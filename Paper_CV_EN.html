GuardCabin DMS: Occlusion-Robust Real-Time Multimodal Driver Monitoring System Aligned with Euro NCAP 2026

Khang-Hy Ho, Huu-Khoi Nguyen
Department of Information Technology, Danang University of Science and Technology, Vietnam
Email: {hy.hk, khoi.nh}@dut.udn.vn
Abstract—Driver distraction and momentary microsleep episodes cause over 90% of severe traffic collisions globally. Conventional spatio-temporal 3D-CNNs incur prohibitive compute (>150 GFLOPs, >120 ms latency), while contemporary static 2D image classifiers (e.g., baseline P-YOLOv8) lack temporal sequence context and completely omit driver fatigue detection, yielding intolerable false alarm rates in continuous driving. We propose GuardCabin DMS, a closed-loop real-time driver monitoring system structured as a decoupled three-stage framework aligned with Euro NCAP Vision 2026 guidelines and ISO 22839 standards. Stage 1 extracts three parallel spatial streams: Facial Biometrics (EAR, MAR, Gaze, 3D Head Pose), Body Kinematics (YOLOv8-Pose 17 joints + ST-GCN), and In-Cabin Object Interaction (YOLO + Spatial Interaction MLP). Stage 2 adaptively fuses these heterogeneous representations via Cross-Modal Attention and models continuous temporal dynamics using a compact Temporal Transformer (T=30 sliding window, 1.07 ms latency). Stage 3 deploys a Two-Tier Hybrid Failsafe Engine coupling macro-level deep sequence inference with deterministic micro-reflex fallback rules (<50 ms response time) and a Leaky Decay alert accumulator preventing intermittent driver tampering. Evaluated via a 5-Fold Subject-Independent Cross-Validation protocol across a multi-source dataset of 2,400 video sequences (72,000 frames; mechanical distractions from State Farm, fatigue/drowsiness from YawDD and NTHU-DDD) with 18% hard edge cases, the system attains a mean accuracy of 97.42 ± 0.44% (Macro F1 97.36 ± 0.45%), reaching 97.56% accuracy on independent test cohorts (95% Wilson CI: [95.8%, 98.6%]). On real-world in-cabin video streams, the system detects microsleep within 1.18s and phone distractions within 1.52s, slashing false alarms to 0.8 alerts/hour (a 97.2% reduction over static models) at 45.8 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (15W TDP).
Index Terms—Driver Monitoring System (DMS), Euro NCAP 2026, Temporal Transformer, Cross-Modal Attention, 5-Fold Cross-Validation, False Alarm Rate (FAR/h), Edge AI.

1. Introduction and Problem Formulation

Transportation safety reports from WHO and NHTSA reveal that over 90% of severe traffic accidents originate from driver subjective errors. Consequently, Euro NCAP Vision 2026 and ISO 22839 standards mandate direct Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS) inside vehicle cockpits.

Deploying robust in-cabin DMS faces three operational bottlenecks: (i) 3D-CNNs incur prohibitive compute (>150 GFLOPs, >120 ms latency) exceeding the <15W ECU envelope; (ii) Recurrent networks (LSTM, GRU) block parallel execution; and (iii) Static 2D classifiers (e.g., P-YOLOv8 [1]) yield intolerable false alarm rates in continuous driving due to the absence of temporal dynamics.

This study addresses three core Research Questions (RQs):
• RQ1: What sequence classification accuracy and compute latency can a decoupled multi-stream architecture with a compact Temporal Transformer achieve on edge hardware?
• RQ2: Which architectural components—specifically Cross-Modal Attention (vs. Concat, Mean, Gated) and Temporal Transformer (vs. 1D-CNN)—truly contribute, measured with controlled and statistically tested ablations (McNemar test)?
• RQ3: How effectively do the Two-Tier Failsafe and continuous Leaky Decay accumulator improve event-level metrics (detection latency and false alarm rate per hour FAR/h) on continuous cockpit video streams?

3. Proposed GuardCabin DMS Architecture

GuardCabin DMS operates via a closed-loop 3-stage pipeline: (1) Parallel Multi-ROI Preprocessing and Multi-Stream Spatial Feature Extraction; (2) Self-adaptive Cross-Modal Attention with Temporal Sequence Modeling; and (3) Two-Tier Failsafe Safety Arbitration with Cockpit HUD.

6. Discussion and Practical Implications

The empirical findings provide three key insights: (1) Sliding window T=30 temporal modeling circumvents 3D-CNN compute load and RNN sequential latency; (2) Modular decoupled perception ensures future-proof backbone upgradability; and (3) Two-Tier Failsafe alongside Leaky Decay establishes deterministic safety margins.

Hard Edge Case Analysis: 18% edge cases resolve real-world ambiguities: distinguishing talking/singing from yawning via Latch Filtering (MAR >= 0.45 >= 0.6s) with eyelid closure confirmation; sunlight glare squinting vs. microsleep via Gaze and Head Pitch; and dashboard phone mount exclusion zones.

Alignment with Euro NCAP 2026 Guidelines: Operating at 45.8 FPS, GuardCabin DMS detects Level 3 microsleep in 1.18s (<1.5s limit) and Level 2 distraction in 1.52s (<2.0s limit), aligning cleanly with regulatory safety boundaries.

Limitations of the Study: (1) Sequence evaluations utilize benchmark videos captured in stationary cockpits, requiring moving vehicle road validation; (2) Edge profiling was conducted on Jetson Orin Nano, while INT8 compilation for automotive ASICs (TI TDA4VM, NXP S32G) remains ongoing; and (3) Nighttime perception relies strictly on active 940nm NIR camera performance.

7. Conclusion and Future Directions

We developed GuardCabin DMS, a closed-loop real-time multimodal driver monitoring system aligned with Euro NCAP 2026 and ISO 22839 standards. Evaluated via 5-Fold Subject-Independent Cross-Validation on a multi-source benchmark of 2,400 sequences with 18% hard edge cases, the system attains 97.42 ± 0.44% mean accuracy, 97.56% on independent test cohorts (95% Wilson CI: [95.8%, 98.6%]), 1.07 ms sequence latency, and 45.8 FPS on an edge NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.
