GuardCabin DMS: Occlusion-Robust Real-Time Multimodal Driver Monitoring System Aligned with Euro NCAP 2026

Khang-Hy Ho, Huu-Khoi Nguyen
Department of Information Technology, Danang University of Science and Technology, Vietnam
Email: {hy.hk, khoi.nh}@dut.udn.vn
Abstract—Driver distraction and momentary microsleep episodes cause over 90% of severe traffic collisions globally according to WHO and NHTSA statistics. Conventional spatio-temporal 3D-CNNs incur prohibitive compute (>150 GFLOPs, >120 ms latency), while contemporary static 2D image classifiers (e.g., baseline P-YOLOv8) lack temporal sequence context and omit physiological fatigue modeling, yielding intolerable false alarm rates in continuous driving. We propose GuardCabin DMS, a closed-loop real-time driver monitoring system structured as a decoupled three-stage framework aligned with Euro NCAP 2026 active safety guidelines and ISO 22839 standards. The system decouples three parallel spatial streams to eliminate confounding background dependencies: Stream 1 (Facial Biometrics: Face ViT-Tiny + MediaPipe 3D Mesh estimating EAR, MAR, Gaze, and 3D Head Pose PnP), Stream 2 (Body Kinematics: YOLOv8n-Pose 17 joints + ST-GCN), and Stream 3 (In-Cabin Interaction: YOLO Cockpit + Spatial MLP identifying phone and bottle interactions). Extracted geometric representations are adaptively fused via Cross-Modal Attention and modeled over continuous temporal dynamics using a compact Temporal Transformer (sliding window T=30 frames, 1.07 ms sequence latency). A standardized multi-source benchmark of 2,400 sequences (72,000 frames; balanced 480 sequences/class across 5 canonical ADAS classes C0–C4) combines mechanical distractions from State Farm synthesized via a Kinematic Pseudo-Sequence Protocol with authentic in-cabin fatigue video streams from YawDD and NTHU-DDD, incorporating 18% hard edge cases. Evaluated via a 5-Fold Subject-Independent Cross-Validation protocol across 62 independent drivers, the system attains a mean accuracy of 97.42 ± 0.44% (Macro F1 97.36 ± 0.45%), significantly outperforming single-frame classification with majority voting (+5.62%, p < 0.001) and 1D-CNN baselines (McNemar test p = 0.038). A Two-Tier Hybrid Failsafe Engine coupled with a Leaky Decay accumulator detects microsleep within 1.18s and phone distractions within 1.52s, slashing false alarms to 0.8 alerts/hour (-97.2%) on continuous video streams while achieving 45.8 FPS benchmarked on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano (15W TDP).
Keywords—Driver Monitoring System (DMS), Temporal Transformer, Cross-Modal Attention, ST-GCN, Two-Tier Failsafe, Leaky Decay, Euro NCAP 2026, Edge AI.

1. Introduction and Problem Formulation

Modern transportation safety analyses conducted by the World Health Organization (WHO) and NHTSA establish that over 90% of severe traffic collisions originate directly from driver subjective errors: cognitive distraction, handheld mobile phone manipulation, fatigue, and momentary microsleep. Consequently, global automotive safety authorities — notably the European Union General Safety Regulation (EU GSR 2024/2026) and Euro NCAP Vision 2026 active safety protocols — mandate Direct Driver Monitoring Systems (DMS) meeting ISO 22839 quantitative latency standards.

Despite substantial advances in computer vision, deploying robust DMS inside automotive cockpits faces three severe operational bottlenecks: (i) Spatio-temporal 3D-CNNs demand >150 GFLOPs with latency exceeding 120 ms, exceeding the <15W thermal envelope of automotive ECUs; (ii) Recurrent architectures (LSTM, GRU) impose sequential recurrence (t → t+1) blocking parallel acceleration; and (iii) Static 2D classifiers (e.g., P-YOLOv8 [1]) achieve high nominal scores on still photos but trigger rampant false alarms in continuous driving (treating a 0.15s natural blink as microsleep, and ear-scratching as phone use).

This study is organized around four primary Research Questions (RQs):
• RQ1: What sequence classification accuracy and compute latency can a decoupled multi-stream architecture with a compact Temporal Transformer achieve via 5-Fold Subject-Independent evaluation on edge hardware?
• RQ2: Does a Temporal Transformer modeling kinematic dynamics significantly outperform single-frame 2D classification with temporal majority voting (Single-frame + Majority Voting) and classic sequence models (1D-CNN, LSTM, GRU), as quantified by McNemar statistical testing?
• RQ3: How much do Cross-Modal Attention (vs. Concat, Mean, Gated) and multi-stream spatial extraction contribute, and does the decoupled architecture guarantee absolute Background-Invariant Representation?
• RQ4: How effectively do the Two-Tier Failsafe and continuous Leaky Decay accumulator improve event-level metrics (detection latency and false alarm rate per hour FAR/h) on continuous cockpit video streams?

Core scientific contributions: (1) A compact Temporal Transformer operating on a FIFO sliding queue of T=30 fused vectors with only 1.07 ms latency; (2) An adaptive Cross-Modal Attention mechanism dynamically compensating between Face, Body, and Cockpit streams under severe occlusions; (3) A Two-Tier Hybrid Failsafe framework coupling deep sequence inference with micro-geometric reflex rules (<50 ms); (4) A continuous Leaky Decay accumulator preventing intermittent driver tampering; and (5) Comprehensive multi-source benchmarking across 2,400 sequences (State Farm + YawDD + NTHU-DDD) via 5-Fold Subject-Independent Cross-Validation, achieving 45.8 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.

2. Related Work and Benchmarking Against Baseline Paper (P-YOLOv8)

Driver monitoring system (DMS) literature has evolved rapidly from early hand-crafted geometric heuristics to deep convolutional and transformer architectures. Most prominently, the recent representative baseline paper by Elshamy et al. (arXiv:2410.15602v1, Oct. 2024) [1] introduced P-YOLOv8, adapting pretrained YOLOv8 classification networks (yolov8n-cls, 1.45M parameters, 4.3 GFLOPs) on the 10-class Kaggle State Farm Distracted Driver Detection dataset (c0–c9), reporting an impressive nominal classification accuracy of 99.46% (F1-score: 99.44%) under a random 70/15/15 train/val/test split.

Scientific Paradigm Shift: While P-YOLOv8 [1] demonstrates high empirical scores on static 2D images, from an automotive ADAS engineering and Euro NCAP 2026 active safety perspective, this benchmark reflects critical task-level limitations: (i) Background Leakage and Subject Overfitting: State Farm comprises 22,424 static photos captured in parked vehicles across only 26 drivers; a random split allows near-duplicate interior backgrounds and driver garments to contaminate test partitions; (ii) Total Absence of Temporal Dynamics: Single-image classification cannot differentiate a harmless 0.15s physiological blink from a lethal >= 1.15s microsleep episode; and (iii) Omission of Physiological Fatigue: The baseline focuses strictly on posture distraction without drowsiness modeling, missing microsleep and yawning. We resolve these limitations via Taxonomy Remapping into 5 canonical ADAS classes C0–C4.

3. Proposed Methodology: GuardCabin DMS

The GuardCabin DMS architecture operates as a decoupled three-stage pipeline: Stage 1 extracts multi-stream spatial features in parallel; Stage 2 adaptively fuses modalities and models temporal sequence progression; Stage 3 enforces safety failsafes and Leaky Decay alert dynamics.

3.1 Stage 1 · Multi-Stream Spatial Feature Extraction

Stream 1 (Facial Biometrics [M2]): MediaPipe 3D Mesh (468 landmarks) measures EAR, MAR, PERCLOS, and 3D Head Pose via PnP. Stream 2 (Body Kinematics [M3-M4]): YOLOv8n-Pose extracts 17 skeletal keypoints modeled via ST-GCN. Stream 3 (In-Cabin Interaction [M5]): YOLO + Spatial MLP tracks handheld phone and bottle interactions.

Background-Invariant Geometric Representation: A cornerstone of the GuardCabin DMS decoupled design is the complete elimination of cabin interior and environmental background pixel dependencies. Rather than feeding raw wide-angle images containing car seats, window views, or vehicle interior decor to the neural network, Stage 1 isolates targeted local ROIs and abstracts them into purely geometric representations: Stream 1 processes a cropped 224x224 facial patch, Stream 2 extracts 17 COCO skeletal keypoint coordinates normalized to driver torso dimensions, and Stream 3 extracts normalized bounding box coordinates and object interaction vectors. By discarding 100% of the surrounding cabin pixels before sequence inference, the model is architecturally precluded from learning spurious background correlations. This guarantees that multi-source integration does not introduce class-dataset confounding.

5. Experimental Results and Benchmarks

5.1 Dataset Origin & Kinematic Sequence Synthesis Protocol

The GuardCabin DMS benchmark comprises 2,400 continuous sequences (T=30 frames, 30 FPS, totaling 72,000 frames) evenly balanced with 480 sequences/class across the 5 canonical ADAS classes C0–C4, integrating State Farm (mechanical distractions) and authentic in-cabin video corpora YawDD and NTHU-DDD (fatigue/drowsiness). To transform static photos into dynamic sequences, we applied a formal Kinematic Pseudo-Sequence Synthesis Protocol: Grouping sequential frames of the same driver exhibiting the same behavior and applying continuous perspective/affine warping drift modeling natural vehicle vibrations coupled with Savitzky-Golay keypoint trajectory smoothing. The dataset incorporates 18% hard edge cases. Table 5 details the dataset architecture.

6. Discussion and Practical Implications

Empirical findings offer three foundational architectural insights: (1) Compact sliding-window temporal modeling over T=30 vectors eliminates 3D-CNN compute overhead and bypasses RNN recurrence bottlenecks; (2) Decoupling the temporal transformer from peripheral detectors guarantees long-term upgradeability; and (3) The Two-Tier Failsafe paired with Leaky Decay establishes deterministic safety bounds while thwarting intermittent driver gaming.

Research Limitations: Several limitations should be recognized: (1) Benchmark sequences were extracted from public datasets (State Farm, YawDD, NTHU-DDD) captured in parked or static cockpit settings; validation on high-speed dynamic road driving with severe road vibrations is required; (2) Edge profiling was conducted on NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano; INT8 quantization on automotive ASICs (TI TDA4VM, NXP S32G) remains for future work; and (3) Complete pitch-black operation relies on active 940nm NIR illumination.

7. Conclusion and Future Directions

We presented GuardCabin DMS, a closed-loop real-time spatio-temporal driver monitoring system aligned with Euro NCAP 2026 active safety protocols and ISO 22839 standards. Validated through 5-Fold Subject-Independent Cross-Validation across 2,400 multi-source sequences with 18% hard edge cases, the system attains 97.42 ± 0.44% mean accuracy (Macro F1 97.36 ± 0.45%) and 97.56% on independent test sets (95% Wilson CI: [95.8%, 98.6%]), with 1.07 ms sequence latency and 45.8 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano.
